Strategies for Maximising Free Tiers

1. Spread your prompts across tools

Don't rely on a single tool. By rotating between ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, and Meta AI, you effectively get dozens of free prompts per day rather than being stuck with one tool's limit.

2. Make every prompt count

Use the RTCF framework (Role, Task, Context, Format) to write specific, detailed prompts. One well-crafted prompt is worth more than five vague ones. For example: "You are an experienced food scientist specialising in fermentation. Analyse the pH trends in my oat milk data across 4 treatments over 48 hours. Present key findings in a table."

3. Use Gemini Notebook as your workhorse

For literature review and research synthesis, Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) is your best tool. Upload your papers, methods articles, and supervisor feedback. It answers from YOUR sources only - no hallucination, generous limits, and it can generate audio overviews for reviewing on your commute.

4. Plan before you prompt

Before opening an AI tool, write down what you actually need. Draft your prompt in a text editor first. This saves wasted prompts on poorly worded requests and helps you think through what you're really asking.

Local AI - Unlimited and Private

If you want unlimited AI access with complete privacy (nothing leaves your laptop), you can run AI models locally. This is especially useful for working with sensitive research data.

Msty

Desktop app with a friendly interface. Runs Llama, Mistral, and other models locally. Can also connect to cloud APIs. Good for beginners.

msty.app

Ollama

Command-line tool for running models locally. More technical but very powerful. Download models with a single command. Pairs well with Msty as a backend.

ollama.com

What do you need to run local AI?

A laptop with at least 8GB of RAM can run smaller models (7-8 billion parameters). For better quality, 16GB+ RAM is recommended. Both Msty and Ollama work on Mac, Windows, and Linux. No internet connection required once the model is downloaded.

Recommended Research Workflow

  1. Start with Gemini Notebook - upload your papers and do source-grounded literature synthesis
  2. Use a chatbot for analysis - upload data, ask "what patterns do you see?", iterate with follow-up questions
  3. Rotate tools when you hit limits - switch from ChatGPT to Claude to Grok as needed
  4. VET everything - Verify, Explain, Test before using any AI output in your research
  5. Consider local AI - if you need unlimited access or work with sensitive research data

For Data Analysis

Upload your CSV files to any chatbot. Start with "What patterns do you see?" then dig deeper with targeted questions. The conversation guides the analysis. Export results to Google Sheets (data), Google Docs (write-up), or Google Slides (figures).
